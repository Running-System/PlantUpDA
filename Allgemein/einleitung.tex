\chapter{Introduction}

The indoor plant market experienced a massive rise during the early 2020s, with consumer spending increasing from \$ 1.31 billion in 2019 to \$ 2.17 billion by 2021 \cite{floristsReviewTrends}. During the same period, household participation in indoor gardening reached a four-year high of 38.1 million households \cite{kunrHouseplants2022}. This development shows that indoor plants have become an important part of many homes.

However, this popularity does not automatically lead to successful plant care. Many plants still fail because users misjudge watering, light conditions, temperature, nutrient availability, or general care requirements. While demand rose by 18 \%, failure rates remained constant: approximately 40 \% of plants perish within the supply chain before reaching a shelf \cite{mordorIndoorPlants}, and another 35 \% die shortly after entering a customer's home \cite{floralDailyKillPlants}. This indicates that the industry's economic "boom" is partly supported by a replacement cycle where consumers repeatedly purchase new plants to compensate for those lost.

This gap between interest and successful long-term care is the starting point of this diploma thesis. The central problem is not the lack of motivation to own plants, but the lack of objective, understandable, and continuous support during everyday care.

Based on this problem, Plant Up! was developed as a smart gardening system that combines sensor-based monitoring with a mobile application \cite{plantUpProject}. Instead of relying only on intuition, users receive measured environmental data and guidance that helps them understand the current state of their plants.

The system monitors relevant factors such as soil moisture, electrical conductivity, light, and temperature in real time. These values are then translated into understandable feedback so that users do not need to interpret raw sensor data themselves. In this way, Plant Up! connects technical measurement with practical plant care.

To make this support useful in everyday situations, the system combines several functions within one structure. These include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Real-time monitoring of soil moisture, electrical conductivity, light, and temperature
    \item Actionable insights and guidance on how to care for plants
    \item A comprehensive database of plants and their care requirements
    \item A community forum for users to share tips and advice
\end{itemize}

The following thesis examines this system from four connected perspectives. First, Arun Sherzai focuses on the sensor-based monitoring approach and explains how environmental conditions can be measured and interpreted for plant care. Second, Abd-Ulrahman Behari analyzes edge and cloud processing strategies for handling sensor data in a smart gardening context. Third, Richard Onuoha examines the backend architecture and data synchronization required to transport and store sensor information reliably. Finally, Dennis Jin investigates how gamification can support long-term user engagement and help users remain consistent in their care routines.

Together, these four parts describe Plant Up! as a complete smart gardening solution: a physical sensor station collects environmental and nutrient-related data, the backend processes and distributes this data, and the mobile application turns it into understandable feedback that supports users in keeping their plants alive.
