\section{Discussion}

The results of the load and stress tests provide an empirical evaluation of the technical trade-offs between Edge and Cloud architectures. The following analysis validates the stability of the \textit{Thin Edge} design within the Plant Up! ecosystem.

\subsection{Validating the ``Thin Edge'' Architecture}

To evaluate the chosen architecture, the empirical latency of the centralized cloud logic was compared against the estimated baseline of localized edge hardware. The results indicate that while a purely edge-centric approach theoretically offers faster localized processing (see Section 5.1), the measured cloud-centric latency is more than sufficient for the requirements of botanical monitoring.

Since plant health parameters change slowly over hours or days, the observed cloud-based latency remains negligible. This architectural trade-off facilitates increased development agility and enables advanced features—such as the AI-driven Daily Mentor—which would be computationally prohibitive to host locally on an ESP32. Ultimately, the structural benefits of the Thin Edge approach outweigh the minor latency considerations for this specific application.

\subsection{Database Performance and Resilience}

The architecture must demonstrate the capacity to handle traffic spikes without service interruptions to ensure backend stability. The stress test benchmarks (see Section 5.3) confirm that the Supabase environment, utilizing the TimescaleDB extension, maintains structural resilience during significant traffic bursts. 

Since Plant Up! does not require synchronous industrial control loops, the asynchronous latency associated with cloud-based ingestion is acceptable. The system demonstrated the capacity to absorb significant simulated loads while maintaining overall database stability and data integrity, further validating the robustness of the gateway pattern.

\subsection{Complexity vs. Operational Maintenance}

The architectural design also involves a balance between hardware autonomy and long-term maintainability. IoT literature suggests that centralizing logic can reduce developmental complexity, although it may increase dependency on network reliability \cite{zeetimThickThin, outsourceThinThick, deepwikiSupabase}. 

Maintaining conditional logic simultaneously in firmware (C++ for the ESP32) and in the cloud (SQL or Edge Functions) can introduce state synchronization challenges. The evaluation suggests that the Supabase backend is sufficiently resilient to support a Thin Edge approach. This allows Plant Up! to utilize simpler device firmware, where modifications to moisture thresholds or gamification algorithms require only a single backend deployment. This approach minimizes the risks associated with over-the-air (OTA) updates and supports rapid feature iteration.

\subsection{Architecture Limitations and Offline Resilience}

A fundamental limitation of employing a purist ``Thin Edge'' architecture is the strict dependency on continuous network availability. In the Plant Up! ecosystem, if the ESP32 microcontroller loses its local Wi-Fi connection, the firmware is intentionally designed to abort telemetry generation. Because the system lacks a localized RAM buffer for caching metrics, the device instead immediately reverts to an Access Point (AP) setup mode, signaling the need for network reconfiguration via onboard LED indicators.

Consequently, any environmental fluctuations occurring during this offline window are lost to the cloud backend. As established previously, this data loss is a calculated trade-off in the consumer botanical context, where parameters like soil moisture change slowly. Missing several hours of granular data has minimal impact on long-term plant health algorithms. This approach prioritizing Wi-Fi over Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) ensures direct device-to-cloud communication without the need for localized gateway devices.

\subsection{Cost vs. Scalability Considerations}

The long-term viability of the Plant Up! infrastructure depends not only on technical performance but also on its financial sustainability and architectural flexibility. By leveraging the Supabase managed platform, the current system successfully operates within a zero-cost free-tier ecosystem, which provides an ideal environment for rapid prototyping and academic validation \cite{supabase_home_2026}. 

As the project scales toward a theoretical deployment of 10,000 active devices, the operational expenses of managed subscriptions—such as the ``Pro'' tier—must be evaluated against the significant engineering overhead required to maintain a custom, self-secured database cluster \cite{supabase_postgresdb_2026, closefuture_supabase_firebase}. A key strategic advantage of this architecture is its inherent resilience against vendor lock-in. Because the backend is built upon an open-source stack wrapping a standard PostgreSQL engine, it remains highly portable across diverse hosting environments \cite{supabase_alternatives, blog_elest_supabase_self_host}. This portability ensures that if managed hosting costs become prohibitive at an enterprise scale, the entire telemetry pipeline and its associated TimescaleDB extensions can be seamlessly migrated via Docker to private bare-metal or cloud infrastructure, confirming that the ``Thin Edge'' model is as economically adaptable as it is technically robust \cite{elestSupabase}.
